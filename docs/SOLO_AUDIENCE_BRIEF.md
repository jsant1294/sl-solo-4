# SnapLink SOLO: how each audience benefits

Date: September 24, 2026. Companion to `SOLO_MARKETING_BRIEF.md`. It covers the eight sample profiles on the homepage demo: who each is for, what problem SnapLink solves for them, the moment it matters, and how they would use it day to day. The claim boundaries in the marketing brief apply here as well (see "Guardrails" at the end).

## The benefit every audience shares

Every audience has the same problem: the moment when someone wants to remember you is short, and a paper card, a spelled-out username, or "I'll send you a link later" loses it. SnapLink turns that moment into one action. They tap your SnapLink or scan its QR code, your profile opens in their phone's browser, and they can save your contact or take the next step right away. The owner keeps the same hardware and updates the profile whenever their life or work changes.

What changes from audience to audience is the next step: saving a contact, watching a reel, reaching a coach or agent, checking a license, or booking a showing.

## SnapTrack (athlete and cheer)

**SnapTrack** is the name for Talent Profiles whose main discipline is athlete or cheer. It means *your journey, on track*: a highlight reel, stats, and milestones that the athlete (or a parent) keeps current season after season.

- Tagline: **"Your journey. Your record. One tap."**
- Say: "your stats, your reel, your journey, all in one tap" and "keep your record current as you grow."
- Don't say: "tracks your stats," "tracks your progress," or "NFC tracking." SnapTrack doesn't collect stats or location automatically; the owner enters and updates everything.
- Many SnapTrack users are minors. Never show maps, location, or monitoring, and show a coach or guardian as the contact.
- Actors, dancers, and multi-talent profiles remain "Talent Profile."

## The eight profiles

### 1. Personal

**Who:** Everyday people: at meetups, new jobs, parties, travel, or church.
**The moment:** "Someone new. One tap: your number and your socials, saved before the moment passes."
**Why it helps:** Nobody has to spell a handle or type a number. The other person saves a complete contact card and follows you in seconds, and you choose what's public.
**How they'd use it:**
- A phone tag on the back of their phone for quick introductions.
- A single profile with contact actions, socials, and a favorite link (such as a playlist, portfolio, or group chat invite).
- Update links as life changes (new job, new city) without replacing the tag.

### 2. Athlete (SnapTrack)

**Who:** Athletes pursuing recruiting or club placement, and the parents who help them.
**The moment:** "Friday night. A college scout in the stands. One tap, and your reel is already playing on their phone."
**Why it helps:** Coaches and scouts see many athletes in a short time. SnapTrack puts the highlight reel first, adds a few key facts (position, class year, measurements the athlete chooses to share), and gives a clear "Contact for recruiting" button that reaches the right person: the athlete, a coach, or a guardian.
**How they'd use it:**
- A bracelet or keychain in the gym bag, ready at combines, camps, and games.
- The latest film as the featured reel, updated during the season.
- A guardian or coach as the contact for minors (the profile supports routing minors' contact to an adult).

### 3. Cheer (SnapTrack)

**Who:** Competitive and school cheer athletes.
**The moment:** "Competition weekend. A college coach walks the mat. One tap shows every stunt you've ever landed."
**Why it helps:** Cheer skills are best understood on video. The profile leads with the skills reel and shows position, level, and class year at a glance, plus a clear contact route for college programs.
**How they'd use it:**
- A bracelet or bag tag worn at competitions and clinics.
- A reel updated after each season, with new skills added as they're landed.
- A combined cheer-and-dance profile if they do both (see Multi-talent).

### 4. Actor

**Who:** Actors at every stage: students, working actors, and those seeking representation.
**The moment:** "The audition ends. The casting director asks, 'Do you have a reel?' You don't dig for a link. You just tap."
**Why it helps:** Actors are judged in short windows. One tap opens the reel, credits, training, and languages, and routes the inquiry to the actor, an agent, or a manager, whichever the actor chooses.
**How they'd use it:**
- An NFC card handed over or tapped after auditions, classes, and showcases.
- A reel, headshot-led profile, credits, and a talent sheet or resume to download.
- An updated contact route once they sign with an agent, without printing anything new.

### 5. Dancer

**Who:** Studio, commercial, and concert dancers.
**The moment:** "After the showcase, a choreographer finds you backstage. One tap: your reel, your training, your agent."
**Why it helps:** Dancers' work is visual and changes fast. The profile keeps the newest reel up front, shows styles and years of training, and gives a booking or representation contact.
**How they'd use it:**
- A phone tag or bracelet at conventions, workshops, and auditions.
- Separate portfolio clips for different styles, with one featured reel.
- English and Spanish content for bilingual opportunities.

### 6. Multi-talent

**Who:** Performers who don't fit one label, such as cheer and dance, or acting and content creation.
**The moment:** "You cheer. You dance. You never fit in one box, and now you don't have to."
**Why it helps:** Instead of separate links for each discipline, one profile shows every discipline together and still gives a single, clear main action, so the viewer isn't confused about how to follow up.
**How they'd use it:**
- One profile with a primary discipline and supporting ones; switching the emphasis later keeps all existing content.
- Different contacts for different goals, such as recruiting in one season and bookings in another.
- A single NFC card or phone tag for every setting.

### 7. Nurse

**Who:** Nurses and allied health professionals looking for their next role or travel assignment.
**The moment:** "Career fair. Twelve booths, one shot. One tap: your license, your resume, and your number in the recruiter's phone."
**Why it helps:** At a career fair, recruiters meet a lot of candidates quickly. A professional profile hands over contact details instantly and links to what recruiters check first: a license verification page and a resume.
**How they'd use it:**
- An NFC card at career fairs, hiring events, and conferences.
- Links to the state license lookup, a resume, and LinkedIn; specialty and certifications in the headline.
- Where the Networking Kit is offered, scanning recruiters' business cards to keep every introduction and follow-up date in one place. (Networking and Resume depend on purchase and configuration; don't imply they're included with every product.)
- **Privacy note for creative:** show professional credentials only, never patient information or a hospital's branding.

### 8. Realtor

**Who:** Real estate agents working open houses, community events, and referrals.
**The moment:** "Open house. Forty visitors. The ones who tap are the ones who call you back."
**Why it helps:** Open houses bring many visitors and few follow-ups. A tap captures the visitor's interest while they're in the home and gives them the agent's listings, a showing link, and text or call in one place.
**How they'd use it:**
- A table stand at the open house entrance and an NFC card for one-on-one conversations.
- Links to current listings (on their brokerage or MLS site), a showing request, and social profiles.
- Updating the featured listing each week without reprinting signage.

## Using this brief for creative

- **Lead with the moment, not the feature list.** Each story line above is the emotional hook: a real, specific situation where the viewer imagines themselves.
- **One person, one product, one receiving phone.** Follow the marketing brief: a believable interaction where the tap and the profile on the phone are clearly visible.
- **Show the right next action on the phone:** a reel for talent, contact and credentials for professionals, "save contact" for personal.
- **Image ideas per audience:** a stadium sideline after a game (athlete), a competition mat (cheer), an audition hallway (actor), backstage after a showcase (dancer), a hiring-event booth (nurse), an open-house entryway (realtor), a coffee line or meetup (personal).

## Guardrails (from `SOLO_MARKETING_BRIEF.md`)

- **SnapTrack** never "tracks" anything automatically. Use "your journey, your record," never "tracking" (see the SnapTrack section above).

- Say **"Tap or scan. Opens in your browser."**, not "works on every phone." NFC needs a compatible phone; the QR code is the alternative.
- "No app required" refers to the person viewing the profile.
- The core profile comes with the hardware. **Networking, Resume, and Talent features depend on purchase and configuration.** Don't imply every product includes every feature. Talent Profile is still being verified for release.
- **Recruiting and casting:** SnapLink shares a reel and routes contact. It doesn't recruit, rank, or endorse talent, and it doesn't guarantee results.
- **Minors:** show a guardian or coach as the contact, and avoid anything that suggests tracking or location.
- Booking and quote links go to **external** tools, and payment links go to external providers.
- The sample names, stats, and achievements are fictional. **Don't use them as testimonials, proof, or usage numbers.**
- Use approved product photography for exact hardware, colors, and finishes.
